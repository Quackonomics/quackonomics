#On the futility of studying economics.

Each night I am doomed to ponder the utter idiocy of this wretched financial circus called "the economy". If I am not doomed by homework, I am doomed by online content mills which thrive on hype, doom and gloom, or speculative euphoria. In the modern world there is no escape from this unceasing rotten cacophony of information. I am constantly under attack from all angles by a never-ending barrage of predictions, warnings and promises, uttered by financial shamans who are no different from ancient men engaging in a futile and ritualistic dance or sacrifice to appease their rain god. In my doom, and in these cursed Dutch winters, I resign myself to the couch and try to stay warm under my late grandma's knitted woolen blanket. The couch promises comfort but delivers none, because it is too short to lie on at full length. For warmth I rely on the blanket, which is scarcely thick enough to properly warm me. During this cursed Dutch winter I have achieved neither comfort nor warmth, but it is better than nothing. If only I had bought a bigger couch, and if only my grandma had made a thicker blanket, I might suffer less. I curse in particular the maker of this couch. Could it not have been made just twenty centimeters bigger? Only a demonic torturer could have designed it. The maker of this couch is an archon who intends to harvest my spiritual dismay and physical discomfort for his own benefit. Perhaps this is another Dutch scheme. Perhaps the maker's intention was to get me to purchase a bigger couch after realizing the mistake of buying one that is just too small. Well, I won't grant him the satisfaction. I would rather sleep in slight discomfort than yield to his capitalist schemes. On this undersized couch I expose myself to the never-ending attacks from financial grifters and various sorts of hylics and charlatans who don't understand what they talk about. They have mastered the art of using many words to say nothing of value. Inevitably, these malicious fools mistake noise for signal, and signal for noise, depending entirely on whatever angle suits their -- or their financiers' -- personal opinions and incentives. In any case, my domestic suffering, courtesy of the couch, the blanket, and primarily the cursed Dutch winter, is a merciful blessing compared to the industrialized suffering induced by this evil monetary circus called "the economy", and the misfortunes of studying it and pondering it. 

In economic studies it is often conveniently ignored that this degenerate circus can only function in the way that it does by virtue of large numbers of humans making the wrong decisions, based on a future they cannot hope to predict. This creates irrational cyclical movements in the prices of equities and assets. These unpredictable price swings cause humans to flip-flop eternally between utter despair and extreme euphoria, when neither are warranted. Why should the price of a stock rise by 400% only for it to fall back down whence it came, within the span of just a few months? Why should a miserable pile of bricks, constructed in 1930, eternally rise in value and outpace inflation? These cursed Dutch houses are built in such a way that the cold winter winds cut right through them. Were winters not cold in 1930? Were couches not too small, and blankets not too thin? Perhaps it is a scheme to sell more natural gas, required to heat these poorly insulated houses. Eskimos were able to figure out insulation long ago, but apparently the Dutch could not consult the laws of physics when building their houses. Roughly 20 years ago, I lived on the 4th floor of such a cursed Dutch apartment, built in the 1950s. This building was populated by pensioners and seniors. Whoever was in charge of the building's heating was even more demonic than the maker of this couch, and I will expose why. In doing so, I will illustrate one facet of the depravity of the Dutch mindset: the total contempt Dutch people have for their fellow Dutch men and women, whom they undoubtedly regard as nothing but pay pigs. The Netherlands is the worst country in the world, but everybody on the outside seems to think otherwise. The Dutch people are so cheap, so depraved, and so morally bankrupt that it defies logic. It certainly defies the rest of the world's perception of this country, which is rich on paper but poor at heart. You see, whoever was in charge of that apartment building's heating plan thought it would be a great and fantastic idea to shut off the heating completely after midnight, regardless of the outside temperature. They likely reasoned that only seniors live there, who go to sleep before midnight; they won't even notice! Indeed, they went to sleep before midnight, like a good Dutch peasant should, and perhaps they really did not notice. In that apartment building built for peasants and seniors, I suffered every winter, because the cold wind would cut right through the brick wall. Thanks to that ice-cold draft I could barely play PC games, let alone study economics, even though studying economics is as futile and pointless as pissing in the ocean and expecting it to change color. This situation carried on for a few years until at once, as if by magic, the heating system suddenly reneged on its refusal to keep heating after midnight. All the suffering of the previous years had been endured for nothing, or endured unwittingly by the seniors who were good peasants and went to bed before midnight. 

This is why the economy can only function when many people make the wrong decisions. For one, nobody should have signed a contract to live in such an apartment, exposed directly to the cold winds of these cursed Dutch winters. Instead, they should have demanded that the apartment be demolished, or formally requested that the German or American air force bomb the building, so as to rectify the post-war mistake of it being built in the first place. Secondly, new couches would not be sold if the one sold to consumers initially was perfect in all ways. Nobody could ever make a living selling couches -- or anything, for that matter -- in a country populated with peasants who are as greedy as goblins and as malicious as mold. If the makers of couches hadn't forced these flat-land trolls and kobolds into making a poor decision, their profits would be cut in half. Finally, if my grandma had made a thicker blanket, I would be warmer today. I, however, refuse to fund Big Blanket's scam. They will not sell me a blanket, no matter how well it might warm me. I will not be swayed by the inferior mass-produced garbage which rolls out of their Chinese factories day after day. Grandma's blanket may not be perfect for a night like this, but it is perfect in every other way, and by not replacing it I am sticking a tiny wrench in the gears of this financial circus called "the economy", which I am doomed to study and ponder, because I am slightly uncomfortable and slightly cold during this cursed Dutch winter, and because I can never escape from the financial grifters and "influencers" who have invaded every last facet of life. I have achieved a minor moral victory, for which I congratulate myself, but I have not learned anything new about economics, which I am doomed to study. Perhaps if I just solve one more equation it will all make sense. As the wind finds another crack in the half-assed brickwork, and as I curse the reality of being imprisoned in the Netherlands, I am once again reminded that we cannot hope to fulfill even our simplest desires without being accosted every inch along the way. I hate the wind, I hate charlatans, and I hate studying "the economy", an inherently dishonest financial circus where all participants are actively encouraged to make the same mistake over and over. The great irony is that academically trained economists fare no better than your average clout chaser with a few thousand subscribers. I am reminded of one teacher who, right before embarking on an explanation of a certain macroeconomic model, jokingly proclaimed it had "...limited predictive value", and so it should be "right up our alley!" Worse than a circus, the economy is a run-down whorehouse, as filthy and oversubscribed as the mistress of the ancient philosopher Rocco, who once famously screamed that he couldn't buy a pack of smokes without running into nine guys she'd fucked. That is the true state of the study of modern economics. We are all standing in the cold, cursed Dutch winter, trying to buy our smokes, surrounded on all sides by men who had, long before we ever had a chance to arrive, already doomed us by turning off the heating, building worthless houses, and peddling us a couch just twenty centimeters too short.
